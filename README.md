# Pascal's hexagon theorem in Lean 4

A Lean 4 + Mathlib formalisation of **Pascal's hexagon theorem** (#28 on Freek Wiedijk's list of
100 theorems), for an arbitrary conic over an arbitrary field, with **Pappus's theorem** as the
line-pair case and **Brianchon's theorem** as its projective dual.

> Let `K` be a field and `Q` a nonzero quadratic form on `K³`. Let `A, B, C, D, E, F` be six points of
> the projective plane `ℙ²(K)` on the conic `Q = 0`. Suppose consecutive vertices are distinct and
> opposite sides are distinct lines. Then the three points `AB ∩ DE`, `BC ∩ EF` and `CD ∩ FA` are
> collinear.

Nothing is assumed about the characteristic of `K`, the nondegeneracy of `Q`, or distinctness of
non-consecutive vertices. The vector form (`pascal_quadraticMap`) holds over any integral domain
and needs no distinctness hypotheses at all.

The mathematics is classical. The key step is the identity
`pascalDet A B C D E F = − det (Veronese 6×6 matrix)`, valid over every commutative ring: six points
lie on a common conic exactly when the 6×6 matrix of their degree-2 monomials is singular.

**Status:** all statements proved, with no `sorry`; `gate.sh` checks 32 named theorems.

## Main statements

| Lean name | File | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| `PascalHexagon.pascal` | `Projective.lean` | Pascal's theorem in `ℙ K (Fin 3 → K)`, with `Projectivization.cross` for lines and intersections and Mathlib's `IsCollinear` |
| `PascalHexagon.pascal'` | `Correctness.lean` | the same, with the representative-independent predicate `OnConic Q P` |
| `PascalHexagon.pascal_of_isCollinear` | `Correctness.lean` | the three intersection points given as *any* points `P, R, S` with `P` collinear with `A,B` and with `D,E`, etc.; no reference to `cross` in the conclusion |
| `PascalHexagon.pappus` | `Correctness.lean` | Pappus's theorem, from the line-pair conic `(a·x)(b·x) = 0` |
| `PascalHexagon.brianchon`, `brianchon_concurrent` | `Brianchon.lean` | Brianchon's theorem: for six lines lying on a nonzero dual conic, the three diagonals are collinear as points of the dual plane, i.e. concurrent. Classically, for a nonsingular conic over a field of characteristic not two, the dual conic is the set of tangent lines |
| `PascalHexagon.pascal_quadraticMap` | `Conic.lean` | vector form over an integral domain: six vectors on a nonzero quadratic map ⇒ the three Pascal points have zero triple product |
| `PascalHexagon.pascal_matrix` | `Conic.lean` | symmetric-matrix form `vᵀMv = 0`, characteristic ≠ 2 |
| `PascalHexagon.det_verMatrix_eq_neg_pascalBracket` | `Veronese.lean` | `det(Veronese matrix) = −[bracket polynomial]` over any commutative ring |
| `PascalHexagon.pascalDet_eq_pascalBracket` | `Bracket.lean` | the Pascal triple product equals the bracket polynomial |

Supporting results in `Correctness.lean` let a reader check that the statement means what it says:
- `mem_line_cross`, `cross_mem_lines`: the line `cross A B` passes through `A` and `B`, and `cross L M` lies on `L` and `M`.
- `eq_cross_of_orthogonal`: uniqueness of lines and intersection points.
- `cross_cross_spec`: `AB ∩ DE` is the unique point collinear with `A, B` and with `D, E`.
- `isCollinear_mk_iff`: three projective points are collinear if and only if their triple product vanishes.
- `onConic_mk_iff`: `OnConic` does not depend on the chosen representative.

## Verification

`./gate.sh` builds the library and generates its own `#print axioms` queries for the 32 named
theorems. The queries are generated by the gate itself, not read from a file in the repository.

The gate fails if any of the following is true:
- a source file contains `sorry`, `admit`, `native_decide`, `axiom`, `#eval`, `run_cmd`, `set_option`, `macro`/`elab`/`syntax`/`notation`, or `import Lean`;
- the build fails;
- a named theorem is missing, or has an extra report;
- a named theorem depends on anything other than `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

Output: `PASS (32 theorems, standard axioms only)`.

`tests/tamper.sh` plants six kinds of fake proof in scratch copies and checks that the gate
rejects each one:

| Test | What it plants |
|---|---|
| sorry | a `sorry` |
| ax2line | an axiom split across two lines |
| evalfake | an `#eval` that prints a fake axiom report |
| macro | a custom macro |
| falsehyp | a weakened statement |
| wrap | a genuine extra axiom with a long name, so the report wraps over several lines |

`python3 code/check_pascal.py` checks the identities numerically with exact rational arithmetic:
random points on random conics, the Veronese identity, the parametric standard conic, and line pairs
(Pappus). Perturbed points serve as a control.

## Related formalisations

Pascal's theorem has been formalised in:
- **HOL Light** (J. Harrison, `100/pascal.ml`): for a real projective conic given by six not-all-zero coefficients, and in affine and circle forms, over ℝ. The statement assumes that 17 triples of the six vertices are non-collinear.
- **Mizar** (R. Coghetto, 2017, article `PASCAL`): in the real projective plane, for a conic given by six not-all-zero real coefficients. It assumes a "Pascal configuration" of 17 non-collinearity conditions.
- **Rocq** (N. Magaud and J. Narboux): in synthetic projective geometry, from axioms that include Pappus.

The development here works over an arbitrary field, including characteristic 2. It assumes only that consecutive vertices are distinct and opposite sides are distinct lines, and its vector form assumes nothing at all. The central identity is classical.

Other Lean 4 developments we are aware of cover circles in ℝ², or rely on an unproved `axiom`, or
contain `sorry`. At the pinned Mathlib version, Pascal's theorem is not in Mathlib.
